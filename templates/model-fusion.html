1. Concepts

Late Fusion

A multimodal technique where different models process different types of data independently, and their final predictions (probabilities) are combined at the end by a Meta-Classifier.

2. Training & Benchmarking

The Meta-Classifier takes the 6 output probabilities (3 from MRI, 3 from Audio) as input features. We tested different algorithms to find the best "decision maker":

Logistic Regression

Used as a linear baseline to see if the probabilities could be separated by a simple hyperplane.

Random Forest

An ensemble of decision trees. It proved to be the best at capturing complex, non-linear relationships between the MRI and Audio probabilities (e.g., "If MRI is highly confident but Audio is not, trust MRI").

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

A distance-based algorithm. It performed poorly because the 6-dimensional probability space is complex and sparse, making distance metrics unreliable.

3. Evaluation Metrics

How was the model evaluated?

  • Global Accuracy: Because the Fusion model outputs a single final diagnosis, the primary metric evaluated was the Global Accuracy (the percentage of total correct predictions across all classes).
  • Cross-Modality Confusion: We analyzed cases where the MRI and Audio predictions conflicted (e.g., MRI says Mild, Audio says Normal). The Meta-Classifier was evaluated on its ability to correctly arbitrate these conflicts based on learned weights.
